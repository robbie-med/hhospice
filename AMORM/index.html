AMORM — Assisted Methadone Opioid Rotation Module

Evidence-based methadone rotation: opioid stack → oMEDD → conversion models → cross-taper schedule with QTc screening.

⚠ Clinical decision support — not a prescribing tool Methadone has a long, variable half-life (8–59 h), non-linear equianalgesic ratios, and a real risk of delayed respiratory depression and QTc prolongation / torsades de pointes. Published conversion models can diverge by a factor of 3 or more at high oMEDD. Every output of this tool must be independently verified and individualised by a prescriber experienced in methadone initiation. This tool does not replace specialist palliative care / pain consultation, and says nothing about whether methadone is appropriate for a given patient.

4Cross-taper schedule

Strategy is independent of the conversion model. “3-day gradual” follows the Edmonton-style thirds taper; “rapid switch” is the stop-and-go approach; “Morley–Makin PRN” is the fixed-PRN titration method.

Evidence base & references

  • Ripamonti C, et al. Switching from morphine to oral methadone in treating cancer pain: what is the equianalgesic dose ratio? J Clin Oncol 1998;16:3216–21. (Tiers 30–90 mg → 4:1; 90–300 → 6:1; >300 → 8:1.)
  • Ayonrinde OT, Bridge DT. The rediscovery of methadone for cancer pain management. Med J Aust 2000;173:536–40. (Tiers <100 → 3:1; 101–300 → 5:1; 301–600 → 10:1; 601–800 → 12:1; 801–1000 → 15:1; >1000 → 20:1; loading dose 25–50% of TDD on days 1–2.)
  • Mercadante S, et al. Switching from morphine to methadone to improve analgesia and tolerability in cancer patients. J Clin Oncol 2001;19:2898–904. (Tiers 30–90 → 4:1; 90–300 → 8:1; >300 → 12:1.)
  • Morley J, Makin M. The use of methadone in cancer pain poorly responsive to other opioids. Pain Rev 1998;5:51–8. (Fixed PRN: 10% of daily oMEDD, max 30 mg, q3h PRN ×5 days; day 6 maintenance = mean of days 4–5, given BID.)
  • Bruera E, et al. / Edmonton protocol 3-day rotation; summarised in Palat G, et al. Practical guide for using methadone in pain and palliative care practice. Indian J Palliat Care 2018.
  • Chou R, et al. Methadone safety: a clinical practice guideline. J Pain 2014;15:321–37. (QTc screening and ECG monitoring recommendations.)
  • McPherson ML. Demystifying Opioid Conversion Calculations, 2nd ed. ASHP, 2018.
  • Parsons HA, et al. A review of common methods to convert morphine to methadone. Can J Hosp Pharm 2012;65(5):355–61 (PMC3715153) — source of the side-by-side model table used here.

MME conversion factors: CDC guideline conversion factors (morphine PO 1; oxycodone PO 1.5; hydromorphone PO 4; codeine 0.15; tramadol 0.1; tapentadol 0.4; fentanyl patch mcg/h × 2.4; buprenorphine patch mcg/h × 12.6; morphine IV/SC × 3; hydromorphone IV/SC × 20; fentanyl IV infusion mcg/h × 7.2). Local formularies (e.g. ANZCA) may differ — verify against your own institution's table before prescribing.

AMORM is free and open-source clinical decision support. No patient data leaves this page — all computation happens locally in your browser. It is not a medical device and is not a substitute for clinical judgement.
